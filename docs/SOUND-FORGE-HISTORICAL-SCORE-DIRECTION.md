### Required two-stage workflow

Stage 1, direction: before notes, commit a versioned independent score brief: narrative purpose, intended duration, musical identity (tonal center/mode or tonal route, meter/pulse, formal design), reference-specific transferable principles, timestamped sections with narrative events, rhythm/accent model, thematic relationship, harmonic intent, register, orchestral roles, foreground/background and spatial depth, transition mechanism, distinctive hook and explicit contrast with recent cues. Inventory required sources with known gaps and a stop condition for unavailable essential material. No note-level score or rendering in this stage. Freeze the brief by commit before implementation. The director need not ask the human to approve every brief.

Stage 2, composition and realization: author original motifs, rhythms, voice leading, counterpoint, phrase structure and cadences; orchestrate before rendering. Compile score against the frozen brief; any changes to direction require explicit brief revision, not retroactive success criteria. Use actual admitted recordings and existing renderer where appropriate. Evaluate timing, expressive phrasing, spectral balance, mono compatibility, stage depth and direct/reverberant balance; preserve stems and original hashes. Separate repeated-note attacks, crossfaded approximate legato and genuine recorded transitions. Human evaluates only one selected new work, not all controls. Metrics do not prove aesthetic quality.

### Diagnose dryness before increasing loudness
1. Rhythm: shared subdivision is necessary but insufficient. Plan accents, pickups, syncopation, rhythmic counterpoint, rests, subdivisions and local tempo shaping, not random jitter. Measure onset concentration, bass/percussion pattern recurrence, distinct rhythmic layers.
2. Orchestration: independent voicing across low/middle/high registers, contrary motion and sustained/short texture contrast. Avoid merely octave-doubling a melody throughout. Prioritize actual contrabass/double-bass, lower brass, timpani/bass drum and ensemble strings/choir only if sources can be verified. No synthetic voice presented as recorded choir.
3. Space: recorded sources often downmixed to mono, static pan and small artificial reflections. Test existing ensemble-room implementation with distinct distance/direct-to-reverb ratio, early reflections and late field, compare mono and transients, preserve native stereo where supported. Don't hide rigid performance under a long reverb.
4. Diversity: compare key/mode, meter, recurring rhythmic cells, phrase lengths, formal architecture, leading instrument and texture against the preceding three works. No forced key rotation alone; meaningful diversity and musical identity matter.
