# Asset Quality & Visual Identity — review only

Historical scope: late medieval Western European foot soldier, c. 1420–1470; padded clothing, mail coif/skirt from the source, a shaped brimmed helmet, wooden ash spear, experimental compact painted wooden shield. The combination is plausible but not certified equipment of a particular unit. Source is the publisher-declared CC0 textured and rigged model https://vibeassets.io/a/bgvqztpyki (15th-century gambeson and mail coif). CI captures actual source hash and attribution; no source bytes were verified locally before downloading. A = retained PBR texture + material-specific equipment; B = modified gear silhouette and contrast; C = painterly palette/edge treatment of A, not a separate sculpt. No claim that the three satisfy the art director's brief yet. Two directions and limited poses only.

Alternative candidates reviewed: https://vinish.dev/free-3d-models/knights (CC BY 4.0 chain mail knight, 23k triangles, 17 bones, appropriate earlier style; no direct source file validated); https://opengameart.org/content/low-poly-psx-style-medieval-soldier-rigged-animated (CC0, existing motions, intentionally low poly; not realistic master); https://literock-studios.itch.io/medieval-spearman (paid, prohibits redistribution of source models; not acquired). Met Museum https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/fashion-in-european-armor-1000-1300 supports earlier nasal helm/mail but is not a 15th-century reference.

Historical correction for deferred antiquity: Polybius 6.23 https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Polybius/6%2A.html describes curved long scutum, sword, pila, bronze helmet, small pectoral or mail depending on wealth. Do not use Imperial segmented armour or later rectangular iconography as Zama's standard. North African elephants probable; species and towers cannot be asserted for every animal: https://www.persee.fr/doc/antiq_0770-2817_2014_num_83_1_3850?pageId=T7_117. No new Roman or elephant asset produced.

The custom workflow uses Universal Art Forge v2 transaction/round-trip contracts in scratch space, retains editable blend, GLB and original PNGs as review evidence. No app production manifest or deployed assets; no change to prior Art Reference, engine, network, AI, save or production renderer. The source is downloaded in CI, not client runtime. Historical and artistic approval remain separate. Failure to acquire or validate the licensed source is a hard error, never replaced by the rejected mannequin.

The browser experiment compares identical states and visual geometry between individual sprite drawing and cached sector composition. The same cached atlas is loaded one style at a time. A/C have limited animation samples; B is a silhouette study, not a complete motion set. This is a bounded infantry study, not a general army renderer.

Limitations: the two direction reference is restricted to opposing infantry; C is a raster treatment rather than a hand-painted texture pass. Tree study uses custom procedural foliage, no claim of photo-quality foliage. At tactical range the row cache may alter inter-row occlusion compared with individual painter sorting; compare actual screenshots before any adoption. Strategic sampling keeps the engine population count separately and is not a count of visual people. Close range retains every living foot figure. The benchmark intentionally uses the same all-spear states, camera and sprite counts for both techniques, not the earlier mixed-weapon benchmark. No additional player scenario is registered.
